Editorial Policy & How We Research

The standards behind every guide on this site, and what we do when something changes or turns out to be wrong.

Our standard for facts

Every factual claim about safety, law, protected areas or wildlife distances should be traceable to an official or well-established source. Our order of preference is:

  1. Government agencies: NOAA Fisheries, the National Weather Service, the Hawaiian Islands Humpback Whale National Marine Sanctuary, the Hawaiʻi Department of Land and Natural Resources (including its Divisions of Aquatic Resources, State Parks and Boating and Ocean Recreation), the Hawaiʻi Department of Health, the Hawaiʻi Tourism Authority, the Hawaii Emergency Management Agency, and county ocean safety and lifeguard services.
  2. Laws and official records: state acts, county ordinances and the Federal Register.
  3. Training and medical bodies: published standards from dive training agencies and guidance from Divers Alert Network and diving medicine organizations.
  4. Established news outlets and dive publications, used for reporting on recent changes and for site descriptions, and named as such.

Main sources are listed at the bottom of each guide. Where sources disagree, or where we could not confirm a detail, we say so on the page or leave the detail out.

What we will not publish

  • Invented prices, opening hours, phone numbers, reviews, ratings or awards. When a price or schedule changes often, we point you to the official page instead.
  • Claims that we visited, dove or tested something unless that is true.
  • Safety advice that contradicts official guidance.
  • Encouragement to approach, touch or feed protected wildlife.

Advertising and sponsored placements

The site is funded by advertising. Automatically placed ads, such as those from Google AdSense, are chosen by the ad network, not by us. Placements for specific businesses are labeled as advertisements or featured placements. Advertisers have no say over the content of our conditions, safety, law, etiquette or sunscreen pages. Directory listings of dive shops are descriptive and are not endorsements of safety records, which we are not in a position to audit. Always confirm an operator's current permits, insurance and reviews yourself.

Some pages link to related travel guides published by the same company. Those links are there to help with trip planning.

Updates and dates

Rules in Hawaiʻi change: reservation systems are introduced, fees go up, protected areas close for recovery and county ordinances are amended. We review safety and law pages regularly and update them when we learn of a change. If a page could not be re-checked recently, the official sources linked on it are the best place to confirm current details.

Corrections

If you find an error, please tell us through the contact page and include the page address and, if possible, a link to the official source. We correct factual errors promptly and do not quietly change safety guidance without checking the source.

Use of images

Photos on this site illustrate diving in Hawaiʻi in general. Unless a caption says otherwise, a photo is not a picture of the exact site or animal named in the nearby text.

How We Build Dive-Site Briefings

Each site briefing on our island pages records the same facts, so that sites can be compared and filtered:

  • Depth range: taken from the state's own description where one exists (for example, DLNR's descriptions of Molokini and Kealakekua Bay), otherwise from established dive publications and operators' published site information. Where sources disagree, we give the range of published figures rather than picking one.
  • Entry and access: shore or boat, the type of entry, the usual departure harbor or parking area, and any reservation or access rule we could confirm.
  • Training: the minimum level shops usually expect, based on standard certification depth limits (about 40 feet for introductory dives with an instructor, 60 feet for Open Water, 100 feet for Advanced Open Water) and the nature of the dive.
  • Protected status: whether the site is in one of the state's Marine Life Conservation Districts, checked against DLNR's published rules.
  • County ocean safety: the county lifeguard agency, with links to Hawaii Beach Safety and the National Weather Service.

How Season Ratings Are Made

The month strips on site cards and the ratings in the Dive Site Finder come from the direction a site faces, matched to Hawaiʻi's long-term swell pattern as described in University of Hawaiʻi surf climatology and reflected in National Weather Service high-surf criteria. "Usually diveable," "swell-dependent" and "usually closed" are tendencies, not forecasts, and every page that shows them says so.

Sponsored Content and Reviews

Our independent guides do not rate or rank dive operators. Where a business has paid for a placement, including a sponsored review, it is labeled as sponsored or as an advertisement, and links to that business are marked as sponsored.

Corrections Log

Substantive corrections, such as a wrong location, a changed rule or a misdescribed experience, are listed with the date on our corrections page. Small fixes such as typos and broken links are made without a log entry.
