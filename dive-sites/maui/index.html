Maui & Lānaʻi Dive Sites

Molokini's crater, the Lānaʻi Cathedrals and Maui's easy shore dives, with the depths, entries, seasons and protected-area rules a guide would brief you on.

Best Diving Hawaiʻi · Updated October 2026 · How we research

What is the best scuba diving on Maui?

Molokini Crater, about three miles off southwest Maui, is the headline: an inside reef at roughly 20–70 ft for all levels and a Back Wall drift for experienced divers, inside a state MLCD where anchoring is banned. Add the Lānaʻi Cathedrals (about 15–60 ft) by boat and shore dives at Five Caves, Ulua Beach and Mala Wharf. Go early, before the trade winds.

Maui's diving is really three islands' worth. Molokini, the sunken crater rim off the south coast, gets the headlines. The lava-tube caverns of Lānaʻi sit across the channel to the west. And Maui itself has some of the friendliest shore diving in the state, from the sand-and-reef bays of Wailea to the turtle-heavy pilings at Mala Wharf.

The trade-off is wind. Maui's isthmus funnels the trades, and by late morning the channel between Maui, Kahoʻolawe and Lānaʻi can be white-capped. Almost every good Maui dive day starts early, and the best shore dives are the ones you are out of the water from before the wind fills in.

The Three Zones of Maui Diving

  • South Maui (Kīhei, Wailea, Mākena). Shore-diving country, with sandy entries at Ulua and the lava arches of Five Caves at Mākena Landing. The coast faces southwest, so it is calmest from autumn through spring and takes summer south swells.
  • The channel (Molokini and Lānaʻi). Boat diving. Molokini lies about three miles off the southwest coast and is a state Marine Life Conservation District. The Lānaʻi Cathedrals are a longer crossing.
  • West Maui (Lahaina to Kapalua). Black Rock at Kāʻanapali, Mala Wharf north of Lahaina and, in summer only, Honolua Bay. Sheltered from most south swell; big winter northwest swells close the northern sites.

Wind, Swell and the Morning Rule

Trade winds dominate Maui from roughly April to October and blow hardest in the afternoon. Boats for Molokini leave Māʻalaea or the Kīhei Boat Ramp early for that reason, and many operators reserve the right to switch to a sheltered backup site when the channel kicks up.

Swell follows the statewide pattern described by University of Hawaiʻi surf climatology: north and northwest swell in winter, south swell in summer. On Maui that means Honolua Bay and other north-facing sites are summer dives, the south-facing Mākena and Wailea coast is at its best in winter, and the south-facing Cathedrals on Lānaʻi take surge on summer south-swell days. The National Weather Service issues a high surf advisory for south-facing shores at 10 feet and north-facing shores at 15 feet.

Winter adds humpback whales. They are in Maui's waters roughly December through April, and on many dives you will hear them sing. Federal rules require staying 100 yards away; whales are not something you dive with.

Molokini's Rules, in Plain Terms

Molokini Shoal is one of Hawaiʻi's Marine Life Conservation Districts, so taking or disturbing marine life, coral or rock is prohibited. The rules that shape your trip are about boats. Commercial snorkel, dive and paddle operations need a permit. Boats tie to state day-use moorings instead of anchoring, and the state's compiled boating rules now prohibit anchoring in the Molokini day-use mooring area. Rules adopted after a 2023 Land Board review added a time limit on mooring use and a monthly mooring fee for commercial operators, which is part of why boats rotate through on a schedule.

For divers, the practical effects are crowds early, a planned dive time and strict no-touch behavior. The inside of the crater is where Discover Scuba students and new divers go; the Back Wall is a drift dive along a face the state describes as dropping past 200 feet, so only experienced divers with solid buoyancy should be there.

Getting to the Boat

Most Molokini trips leave from Māʻalaea Harbor, which has paid parking within a short walk of the docks; allow time to find a space. Smaller six- and twelve-passenger boats use the Kīhei Boat Ramp on South Kīhei Road, the closest departure point to Molokini, with upper-level lots for visitors. Lānaʻi trips leave from Māʻalaea or, again, West Maui.

For shore dives, arrive early. Mākena Landing's lot is small, the Wailea beach-access lots fill by mid-morning, and roadside parking for Honolua Bay is along a busy highway. Lock valuables out of sight or leave them at your accommodation.

West Maui After the 2023 Fire

The August 2023 wildfire destroyed much of Lahaina town and badly damaged Lahaina Small Boat Harbor, displacing dozens of ocean businesses; state reporting says 12 of 48 displaced companies moved to Māʻalaea. The harbor reopened to limited daytime commercial operations on December 15, 2025, with recovery work expected to continue through 2027. If a West Maui shop lists a Lahaina meeting point, confirm where the boat actually leaves from, and treat the town with the respect owed to a community that is still rebuilding.

Lānaʻi's Cathedrals Without the Hype

First Cathedral is a two-level lava-tube chamber whose openings let in shafts of light, roughly 25 to 60 feet deep. Second Cathedral is shallower at about 15 to 50 feet, with more swim-throughs. They are caverns with daylight visible, not cave dives, but you are under a ceiling and surge can push you around inside. Good buoyancy, a calm day and a guide who knows the routes make the difference between a highlight and a scramble.

Safety, Sunscreen and Haleakalā

Lifeguards on Maui belong to the county's Department of Fire and Public Safety, Ocean Safety Bureau, which guards a limited number of beaches. Most shore-dive entries have no tower. Check Hawaii Beach Safety and the NWS Honolulu surf forecast before you go.

Maui County has the strictest sunscreen law in the state: since October 1, 2022, only mineral sunscreens (zinc oxide or titanium dioxide as the active ingredients) may be sold, distributed or used without a prescription. See our sunscreen rules.

The biggest Maui-specific risk is altitude. The summit of Haleakalā is 10,023 feet, and the drive up from sea level takes a couple of hours. That is higher than the cabin altitude of an airliner, so treat a post-dive sunrise trip as seriously as a flight. Our flying and altitude guide sets out the Divers Alert Network intervals and a simple plan: do Haleakalā first, dive after.

On Maui, the best dive plan starts with an alarm clock. Be on the boat early and you get the calm water and the empty mooring.
