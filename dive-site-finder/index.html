Which Hawaiʻi dive sites work in my month?

Use the finder below. In winter (about Nov–Mar), favor south-facing coasts and the Kona coast; north shores such as Shark's Cove, Honolua Bay and Tunnels usually close. In summer (May–Sep), north shores open and south shores take occasional swell. The Kona coast and Molokini dive in most months.

Pick an island, the month you will be there and your level of training. The finder returns the sites from our island guides that suit you, sorted so the ones that are usually diveable that month come first and the swell-dependent ones follow. Sites that usually close that month, or that need more training than you selected, are left out.

How the Finder Decides

The finder uses the same data as our island dive-site pages. Every site carries four facts that matter here:

  • Island and entry. Whether you walk in from shore or need a boat.
  • Training. The minimum level a shop will usually expect: snorkel, Discover Scuba with an instructor, Open Water (about 60 feet) or Advanced Open Water (about 100 feet). Choosing a level includes every site at or below it.
  • Exposure. Which way the site faces and so which swell reaches it: north-facing, south-facing, west-facing, the sheltered Kona coast, the Molokini channel, or the long open-ocean crossing to Niʻihau.
  • Protected status. Whether the site is inside a state Marine Life Conservation District.

Exposure is turned into a month-by-month rating using the long-term swell pattern described in University of Hawaiʻi surf climatology and reflected in National Weather Service high-surf criteria: north and northwest swell in winter, south swell in summer, and a sheltered leeward coast on Hawaiʻi Island.

Usually diveableSwell-dependentUsually closed / not run

What the ratings mean. "Usually diveable" means the site faces away from that season's main swell, so operators run it on most days. "Swell-dependent" means it is diveable on calm days but a swell in the forecast will close it. "Usually closed" means the site faces straight into that season's surf and should not be planned around. None of these is a forecast. Always check the NWS Honolulu surf forecast and Hawaii Beach Safety in the days before you dive, and follow your operator's call on the morning.

Using the Results Well

Treat the list as a shortlist to take to a dive shop, not a booking decision. Three habits get the most out of it:

  1. Look one level up and one level down. If you are a new Open Water diver, switch the training filter to "Not certified" to see the calmest, shallowest options for your first day back in the water. If you are logging your Advanced dives, switch up to see what opens.
  2. Check the shoulder months. April and October are transition months for north-facing sites. If your trip straddles them, check both months and plan the north-shore dives for whichever end is closer to summer.
  3. Keep a backup on another coast. Each island has at least one site that tends to work when the others do not: the Honolulu wrecks in winter, Mala Wharf on Maui, Koloa Landing on Kauaʻi, and the Kona coast almost any time.

Before you lock anything in, read the full briefing for each site you like, including access and parking, county ocean safety and the rules inside protected areas. If a summit drive is on your list, read the flying and altitude guide too.
