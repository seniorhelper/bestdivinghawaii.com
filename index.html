What is the best diving in Hawaii?

Maui's Molokini Crater is Hawaii's #1 dive site — a submerged volcanic caldera offering up to 150 feet of visibility and 250+ marine species, accessible only by boat 2.5 miles off the coast. For a once-in-a-lifetime experience, the Kona Manta Ray Night Dive off the Big Island draws Pacific manta rays with 14-foot wingspans to feed in graceful barrel rolls inches from divers — consistently ranked among the top 10 dives on the planet. On Oahu, our top-rated operator pick is Honolulu Scuba Company for safety-first boat dives to turtles, eels, and reefs. Hawaii offers world-class diving year-round across 6 islands, with water temperatures from 72–82°F, 700+ fish species (25% found nowhere else on Earth), sea turtles, sharks, monk seals, and spectacular wreck and cave diving.

Night Diving Hawaii

Hawaii Night Diving:
What to Expect

Night diving in Hawaii reveals a completely different world. Hunting octopus, luminous nudibranchs, and shy species that hide during the day all emerge under torch light. The Kona manta ray night dive is the pinnacle, but Maui, Oahu, and Kauai all offer excellent night dive operations.

  • Best night dive: Manta Ray Night Dive, Kona (beginner-friendly)
  • Best advanced night dive: Pelagic Magic, Kona open ocean
  • Night diving season: Year-round (summer has calmest conditions)
  • Required gear: Primary + backup dive lights, SMB marker buoy

Shark Diving Hawaii

Shark Diving in Hawaii:
The Complete Guide

Hawaii is home to over 40 shark species, making it one of the Pacific's premier shark dive destinations. The most popular experience is the cage-free dive off Haleiwa on Oahu's North Shore, where Galapagos and sandbar sharks gather in numbers that can reach 100+. These are reef sharks — curious and predictable in the company of experienced guides.

  • Best location: Haleiwa, Oahu North Shore (cage-free)
  • Species: Galapagos shark, sandbar shark, whitetip reef shark
  • Best season: Year-round (summer has clearest visibility)
  • Certification required: Open Water minimum

Best Scuba Diving Maui

Best Scuba Diving in Maui:
Sites, Wrecks & Conditions

Maui's reputation in the dive world rests almost entirely on Molokini Crater — and rightfully so. But the island offers far more: the lava arches of Five Caves at Makena, the Pinnacles seamount, and the Corsair — a WWII Vought F4U fighter plane resting at 100 feet, one of Hawaii's most dramatic wreck dives.

  • Top site: Molokini Crater (all levels, boat-only, AM trips best)
  • Best wreck: Corsair WWII fighter plane (100ft, advanced)
  • Unique site: Five Caves / Five Graves lava tube system
  • Departures: Maalaea Harbor and Kihei boat ramps

Liveaboard Hawaii

Hawaii Liveaboard Diving:
Multi-Day Dive Expeditions

Hawaii's liveaboard options are more intimate than Maldives or Red Sea fleets, but they access dive sites simply unreachable on day boats. Multi-day trips around the Big Island and inter-island passages to Maui visit offshore pinnacles, seamounts, and rare deep-water environments that define truly world-class diving.

  • Most popular route: Kona to Maui inter-island passage
  • Duration: 3–7 nights typical; book far in advance
  • Sites accessed: Remote offshore seamounts, deep pinnacles
  • Certification: Advanced Open Water strongly recommended

PADI Open Water (or equivalent) certification is required for independent scuba diving. However, uncertified beginners can take a Discover Scuba Diving (DSD) introductory experience with a certified instructor, reaching 40 feet without prior certification. Full certification courses are available on every major Hawaiian island, typically completed in 3–4 days.
Hawaii's waters host an extraordinary range: Hawaiian green sea turtles (honu), Pacific manta rays, Hawaiian monk seals (critically endangered), spinner dolphins, reef whitetip and Galapagos sharks, spotted eagle rays, day octopus, tiger moray eels, frogfish, and 700+ fish species — including 25% found nowhere else on Earth.
Hawaii's ocean temperatures range from 72°F (22°C) in winter to 82°F (28°C) in late summer. A 3mm full wetsuit is recommended year-round for comfort during multiple dives. A 5mm is preferred at depth or during winter. Snorkelers often use a rash guard or 1–2mm short wetsuit in summer.
The Kona manta ray night dive off the Big Island is one of the world's most remarkable dive experiences. Divers kneel on a sandy bottom at 25–30 feet while holding underwater lights that attract plankton. Pacific manta rays — some with wingspans exceeding 14 feet — perform graceful barrel rolls to feed on the plankton, sometimes just inches from divers' faces. It runs year-round, 4–5 nights per week.
Hawaii is one of the best beginner diving destinations in the world. Calm, warm, clear water; hundreds of qualified dive operators; and protected sites like Hanauma Bay (Oahu) and Hulopo'e Bay (Lanai) create ideal conditions for new divers. Most operators offer same-day Discover Scuba experiences — no prior training required.
Maui wins on pure dive quality — Molokini Crater, Five Caves, and Makena Landing offer superior visibility and marine life density. Oahu offers greater convenience, more operators, popular wreck dives (YO-257), and the best shark dive experience. Serious divers typically choose Maui; those prioritizing ease and variety often prefer Oahu.
Hawaii is globally renowned for exceptional underwater visibility. Molokini Crater tops 150 feet in peak season. Most leeward sites across the islands average 60–100 feet year-round. Windward and north-facing sites may drop to 40–60 feet during winter swell season. Kona on the Big Island stays sheltered with excellent visibility year-round.

The inner crater offers 30–60 foot depths ideal for beginners and snorkelers; the outer wall drops 300+ feet for advanced divers. Morning trips offer the calmest conditions and best visibility.
Yes. Several operators offer multi-day liveaboard trips around the Big Island, Maui, and inter-island passages. For most Hawaii dive sites: a 3mm wetsuit, properly fitting mask, fins, BCD, and regulator (all rentable from operators), a dive computer, and an underwater light for cave or night dives. All gear is available for rental across every island. Bringing your own mask ensures a reliable fit — the single most common comfort issue on charter dive boats.
